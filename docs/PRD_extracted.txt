Product Requirements Document (PRD)

University Staff Self-Service Portal

Institution: Nigeria French Language Village (NfLV), Badagry
Version: 1.0
Date: 13 October 2026
Initial scale: Approximately 500 staff users
Product type: Secure, responsive web application

1. Executive Summary

The Nigeria French Language Village (NfLV), Badagry, requires a secure, cen-
tralised Staff Self-Service Portal that digitises staff correspondence and adminis-
trative services involving the Registry, Bursary, ICT Unit, Heads of Department
and institutional management.

The portal will provide each staff member with a personal account through which
they can receive official memos, manage their profile, access monthly payslips,
apply for leave, track approvals, download official letters and communicate with
relevant administrative units.

Authorised officers will use dedicated administrative dashboards to manage staff
records, distribute correspondence, process applications, publish payslips, gen-
erate official letters and monitor outstanding requests.

The product will be developed as a responsive web application, using the institu-
tion's existing technical expertise and institutional email infrastructure wherever
practical.

1.1 Product Vision

Establish one trusted digital platform through which staff can access autho-
rised personnel information and complete routine administrative transactions
without depending on paper-based correspondence or repeated physical visits
to administrative offices.

1.2 Business Objectives

   · Reduce paper-based memos and routine administrative correspondence.
   · Improve the speed, traceability and accountability of leave approvals.
   · Provide secure, timely access to payslips and personal official letters.
   · Reduce manual work for Registry and Bursary officers.
   · Establish a central, searchable record of staff correspondence and admin-

       istrative transactions.
   · Improve staff access to services from campus or off campus.

                                                   1
· Provide management with reliable reports on applications, correspondence
   and administrative workloads.

1.3 Product Scope

The initial product will include:

   1. Public landing page and secure staff login.
   2. Staff account and profile management.
   3. Role-based access control.
   4. Memo and circular distribution.
   5. Payslip publication and access.
   6. Leave applications and approvals.
   7. Official letter generation and delivery.
   8. Registry and Bursary service requests.
   9. Email and in-portal notifications.
  10. Administrative reports, audit trails and system configuration.

The portal is not intended to replace the institution's existing payroll calculation
system, email hosting service or public website in the first release.

2.1 Permission Principles

   · A staff member may access only their own private records unless an addi-
       tional authorised role grants access.

   · Bursary personnel may access payroll data only to the extent required by
       their assigned duties.

   · HR/Registry personnel may manage personnel records but must not au-
       tomatically gain unrestricted payroll access.

   · ICT administrators may manage technical operations without automati-
       cally acquiring permission to approve leave or read confidential correspon-
       dence.

   · Approvers may decide only on requests routed to them under the config-
       ured workflow.

   · Privileged access must be individually assigned, reviewed periodically and
       revoked when no longer needed.

   · Emergency or exceptional access to confidential records must be restricted,
       justified and audited.

3. Product Architecture and Navigation

3.1 Public Landing Page

The unauthenticated landing page shall introduce the Staff Portal and provide
a clear entry point for staff login.

Required components:

   · NfLV logo, name and official branding.
   · Welcome heading and concise portal description.
   · Service highlights for correspondence, payslips, leave and profile manage-

       ment.
   · Staff ID or institutional email login.
   · Password field with optional visibility toggle.
   · Forgot-password link.
   · Login button with loading and error states.
   · Contact information for ICT support.
   · Privacy notice, terms of use and institutional footer.

                             3
   · Responsive design for desktop, tablet and mobile.
   · Accessible contrast, keyboard navigation and readable text.

The page must not display private staff records, internal memos, payslips or
administrative reports before authentication.

3.2 Authenticated Staff Dashboard

The dashboard shall display:

   · Staff name and profile summary.
   · Unread memos and recent correspondence.
   · Latest available payslip.
   · Leave balance, subject to the institution's approved leave rules.
   · Active leave applications and their statuses.
   · Pending actions and requests for corrections.
   · Recent letters and downloadable documents.
   · Notifications and links to Registry/Bursary services.

3.3 Administrative Navigation

Administrators shall see navigation items appropriate to their permissions, such
as:

   · Dashboard and reports.
   · Staff directory and personnel records.
   · Memo and correspondence management.
   · Leave applications and approval queues.
   · Payslip imports and publication.
   · Letter templates and document issuance.
   · Service requests and enquiries.
   · User roles and permissions.
   · Audit records and system settings.

Hiding a navigation item is not a security control; each action and protected
record must also be authorised on the server.

4.8 Notifications

The system shall provide:

   · In-portal notifications for new memos, payslips, approvals, rejections and
       service-request responses.

   · Email notifications through the institution's existing email infrastructure.
   · Notification preferences where permitted by institutional policy.
   · Retry and failure logging for unsuccessful email deliveries.
   · Queue processing for bulk notifications, avoiding long delays during nor-

       mal user requests.
   · Generic notification content for sensitive matters, directing users to sign

       in rather than including confidential details in email.

An email delivery failure must not erase the underlying portal record or falsely
indicate that the email was delivered.

4.9 Administration and Reporting

Authorised administrators shall be able to:

   · View dashboard summaries appropriate to their role.
   · Search staff records within their authorised scope.
   · Monitor pending leave applications and unresolved service requests.
   · Review memo publication and acknowledgement statistics.
   · View payslip batch reconciliation results.
   · Generate approved operational reports.
   · Export permitted reports using controlled access and audit logging.
   · Configure approval rules, document templates and notification settings

       within their authority.

5.1 Security Standards and Controls

The implementation shall incorporate:

   · Protection against common web application risks, informed by the
       OWASP Top 10.

   · Secure password hashing using framework-supported algorithms.
   · Multi-factor authentication for privileged users.
   · Least-privilege role assignments and resource-level authorisation.
   · CSRF protection, output escaping, input validation and safe database

       queries.
   · File upload restrictions, including allowed file types, size limits and mal-

       ware scanning where available.
   · Private storage for payslips and confidential attachments.
   · Rate limiting and monitoring of suspicious authentication activity.
   · Encrypted backups and appropriate protection of data in transit and at

       rest.
   · Controlled production access, secret management and timely security up-

       dates.
   · A documented security incident response process.

The institution should assess its obligations under the Nigeria Data Protection
Act 2023 and applicable Nigeria Data Protection Commission requirements, in-
cluding appropriate notices, access controls, retention, incident handling and
any required data-protection impact assessment.

6.1 Data Design Rules

   1. Every staff member shall have a unique institutional staff number.
   2. Staff accounts shall reference personnel records through stable identifiers

       rather than names.
   3. Every private document shall have an explicit owner or authorised recipi-

       ent set.
   4. Approval decisions shall be preserved as historical records rather than

       overwritten without trace.
   5. Payslip publication and leave approval shall use database transactions

       where multiple related records must change together.
   6. File contents shall be stored in private storage, not directly in publicly

       accessible web directories.
   7. The system shall distinguish between soft deletion, account deactivation

       and formal records disposal.
   8. Access to audit logs shall be restricted and protected against unauthorised

       alteration.
   9. Retention and disposal rules shall be configurable according to approved

       institutional policy and legal requirements.

8. Document Generation Requirements

Official letters must use institution-approved templates and a consistent docu-
ment identity.

Each issued letter should contain, where applicable:

   · Institution name, logo and official heading.
   · Unique reference number.
   · Issue date and recipient details.
   · Subject and approved letter content.
   · Authorised signatory's name and designation.
   · Approval or signature information supported by the system.
   · Appropriate classification or confidentiality marking.
   · A document version or verification reference, where required.

The institution shall determine whether electronic signatures, scanned signa-
tures or a digitally verifiable approval mechanism are acceptable for each doc-
ument type. A scanned signature image alone must not be treated as proof of
document integrity or authenticity.

9. User Experience and Accessibility

The portal shall provide a consistent interface across public, staff and adminis-
trative areas.

9.1 Design Requirements

   · Use official NfLV branding and an institution-approved colour palette.
   · Display a clear primary action on each page.
   · Use plain language for application statuses and validation errors.
   · Provide confirmation screens for important actions.
   · Distinguish clearly between drafts, submitted requests, approvals and re-

       jected requests.
   · Show loading, empty, success and error states.

· Support keyboard navigation, visible focus indicators and accessible form
   labels.

· Avoid relying on colour alone to communicate status.
· Confirm before destructive or irreversible actions.

9.2 Required Status Labels

The system should use a consistent set of statuses, including:

   · Draft
   · Submitted
   · Pending review
   · Pending approval
   · Returned for correction
   · Approved
   · Rejected
   · Published
   · Withdrawn
   · Cancelled
   · Closed

Only statuses relevant to a particular module should be shown to users.

10.2 Application Modules

The codebase should be organised into distinct modules or clearly separated
application domains:

   1. Identity and access management.
   2. Staff directory and profile management.
   3. Correspondence and memo management.
   4. Payroll document distribution.
   5. Leave management.
   6. Workflow and approval engine.
   7. Document templates and issuance.
   8. Service requests.
   9. Notifications.
  10. Reporting and audit.
  11. System configuration.

A modular monolith is recommended for the first release. It avoids the oper-
ational complexity of microservices while preserving clear boundaries between
business functions.

10.3 Integration Requirements

The initial release shall integrate with:

   · The institutional email system for notifications and recovery messages.
   · Existing personnel records through controlled import.
   · Existing payroll outputs through approved file imports.

Potential later integrations include institutional single sign-on, payroll APIs,
digital signature services and existing HR information systems.

Every integration shall have defined permissions, error handling, data validation
and operational ownership. No integration should require sharing an adminis-
trator's personal credentials.

12.1 Pilot Group

Start with approximately 20-30 users from ICT, Registry, Bursary and selected
departments. Include ordinary staff, supervisors, approvers and administrative
users.

The pilot shall test:

   · Staff onboarding and account recovery.
   · Memo publication and access restrictions.
   · Payslip upload, reconciliation and private access.
   · Leave submission, review and approval letters.
   · Profile update requests.
   · Email failures and notification retries.
   · Audit logs and backup restoration.

Use synthetic payroll and personnel data during development and demonstra-
tions. Production data may be introduced only after authorisation and the
required security checks.

12.2 Staff Onboarding

After successful pilot sign-off:

   1. Import and reconcile the authorised staff register.
   2. Verify staff numbers, departments and institutional email addresses.
   3. Issue account activation instructions securely.
   4. Train administrative officers and approvers.
   5. Publish user guidance and ICT support contact details.
   6. Onboard staff in controlled batches.

15. Out of Scope for the Initial Release

The following capabilities are excluded unless separately approved:

   · Payroll calculations and salary disbursement.
   · Full accounting or financial management.
   · Recruitment and applicant tracking.
   · Student information management.
   · Library, hostel and student payment systems.
   · A native Android or iOS application.
   · Automatic electronic signatures without an approved signature process.
   · Replacing the institution's existing email hosting service.
   · Unrestricted editing of official personnel or salary records by staff.

The architecture should allow future integrations without requiring these sys-
tems to be built into the first release.

18. Definition of Done

The initial portal shall be considered ready for production when:
   1. All P0 requirements are implemented and tested.
   2. All critical staff journeys work from login through completion.
   3. Role and document-access restrictions pass negative testing.
   4. Payslip imports and leave approvals pass end-to-end testing.
   5. Required email notifications, retries and failure logs are operational.
   6. Official document templates have been approved.
   7. Backup restoration and incident response procedures have been tested.
   8. No unresolved critical or high-severity security findings remain.
   9. User documentation and administrator training are complete.

  10. The pilot has passed its acceptance criteria and authorised institutional
       representatives have signed off.

This PRD provides the product requirements and proposed delivery bound-
aries. The next deliverable should be a Software Requirements Specifica-
tion (SRS) and technical design package containing:

   · Detailed database schema and entity relationships.
   · Complete role-permission matrix.
   · Laravel project folder and module structure.
   · API and integration specifications where required.
   · Screen-by-screen wireframes for the landing page, staff dashboard and

       admin dashboards.
   · Detailed approval and document-generation rules.
   · Test cases mapped to each requirement.
   · Deployment, backup and security configuration.
That package will give the IT team a practical implementation guide and reduce
the risk of building features that do not match the institution's actual Registry,
Bursary and management processes.
